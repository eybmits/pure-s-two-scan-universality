\lead{The scan delay}
For $u=\wrap{w}$ let $q=Su$, $v=qq$, and let $r(z)$ be the normal form of $zq$ for a wrapped suffix $z$ of $u$. After $3+\#_A(w)+2\#_B(w)$ rounds of preparation the scan is $r(u)v$. Its two routes follow from
\[
\mathcopy{r(Az)v \unichar{8594} (qv)(r(z)v),\unichar{10}r(Bz)v \unichar{8594} (r(z)v)((q r(z))v),\unichar{10}(q r(z))v \unichar{8594} (uv)(r(z)v),\unichar{10}r(Sg)v \unichar{8594} (gv)(qv).}{%
\begin{aligned}
 r(Az)v      &\step (qv)(r(z)v),\\
 r(Bz)v      &\step (r(z)v)((q\,r(z))v),\\
 (q\,r(z))v  &\step (uv)(r(z)v),\\
 r(Sg)v      &\step (gv)(qv).
\end{aligned}}
\]
At $B$ the direct continuation is the left child after one round. In the next round it advances, while the right branch splits into a fresh scanner $uv$ on the left and the delayed continuation $r(z)v$ on the right. Each earlier letter costs one round, plus one extra for each delayed $B$. Thus for a probe $B$ at position $i$, after $d$ such delays and its own two launch rounds, the scanner is born at $i+d+2$ (\decl{Frames.scannerBirth}). From birth it peels one wrapper per round, since $(Az)v\step Sv(zv)$ and $(Bz)v\step(Av)(zv)$. At $A$ the left marker is normal and the right continuation is active. Shape and normality are finite-state tests.

\lead{Exact read position}
The finite reader follows the right branches of the peeled wrappers. It tests for an operand of shape $(S\wrap{w_1})(S\wrap{w_2})$ without comparing $w_1$ and $w_2$. In this scan every match equals $v$. A new application contains $v$ in its right child, which the shape $S\wrap{w_2}$ excludes. Let $j=t-i-d-2$ be the scanner's age. A probe reads the suffix beginning at $j$ exactly when $0\leq j<|w|$.

Every candidate word on an encoded machine orbit ends in the input suffix $\sigma$, whose final block $[V_b]$ ends in $B$. After this last wrapper peels, set $h=(Sg)v$. The innermost frame takes the step $(Av)h\step(Sh)(vh)$. The general exhaustion argument works for any last letter and for the empty word. Once all wrappers have peeled, $h$ is normal and every remaining redex has a right operand larger than $v$. Contractions preserve this bound, so the reader can never again match an active site with operand $v$.
