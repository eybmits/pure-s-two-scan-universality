\papersection{Reading a run in order}\label{sec:chronology}
What can the tree reveal about a computation that never halts? Fix a deterministic machine with one semi-infinite tape and absorbing halts. Write $C_i$ for its configuration after $i$ steps, omitting the right boundary marker and trailing blanks beyond the head. Choosing the universal machine of Section~\ref{sec:history} fixes one reader for every program and input.

\lead{Finite prefixes in parallel rounds}
Allow any head state in the final row. A fixed finite tree automaton detects whether the tree contains a passing packet. Each such packet gives the true rows through its final step. For any $h$ and round bound $B$, pad a tableau through $C_h$ until its word is larger than every tree through round $B$. The generator eventually produces this packet, necessarily after $B$. Reading a passing packet with the most rows (first in preorder on ties) therefore recovers $C_0,\ldots,C_h$ together, even on a nonhalting run.

\begin{samepage}\begin{corollary}[Chronological readout]\label{cor:chronology}
Freeze each round's initial innermost redexes and contract them from left to right. A linear-size seed and a fixed reader of the current tree give $(i,C_i)$ at times $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots$. Every reported pair is correct for its index. The increasing times select one observation per index.
\end{corollary}\end{samepage}

The reader compares unbounded row counts and counts differing clock sites, operations beyond the finite-state detector. The seed pairs the data and literal initial row with two copies of the empty-suffix generator. During the first copy's sweep the second copy and data stand still. After $i+1$ contractions the copies differ at $i+1$ original redex sites, directing the reader to row $i$ of a longest passing packet. While the second copy advances, the data still form a round snapshot. While the data advance, equal clocks give $(0,C_0)$. Each renewal leaves another disjoint active subtree, so every sufficiently late sweep has more than $i$ sites. Arbitrarily late snapshots cover $i$. Taking the first subsequent report of each next index recovers the run online.

{\raggedright\papersection{Formal verification}\label{sec:formal}
Lean~4.35.0 and Nanoda~0.4.19 checked nine theorems. The universality and hardness statements name their encoders and readers. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is \decl{Certified.universality}. The three-copy result is \decl{Majority.universal_witness}. The numerical program--input result of Section~\ref{sec:comparison} is \decl{OrbitHardness.concrete_indexed}.

The two-copy theorem is \decl{Prize.serial_universal_witness}. Test both at the seed and after each contraction, and return the left output when both first pass. If this happened midway through the left sweep, the right copy would still be at a passing boundary. Both copies would already have passed at the start of the sweep. Thus the first hit reads the left copy at a boundary. At any snapshot, testing its output against a regular language combines a regular test on the left with a regular domain test on the right.

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} uses \decl{propext}, \decl{Classical.choice} and \decl{Quot.sound}. The export admits only these axioms. Lean checks regularity, seed and output sizes, and structural traversal bounds. Section~\ref{sec:comparison} derives the implementation times from those bounds and a fixed number of linear passes.

The embedded and standalone copies of \texttt{pure-s-lean-proof.zip} are identical. Start with \decl{TCB_AUDIT.md} for definitions, theorem statements and replay commands. The Python example starts from a prepared two-cell packet. It runs the scans on both a valid history and a forged update. Detection from the universal seed is established by the proof.
